# Ecosystem Aggregator Model

**Dimension(s):** D: Ecosystem (D1 Ecosystem design, D3 Coordination mechanism, D4 Network operator)
**Type:** Pattern

## What this is

The ecosystem aggregator model is a strategy for rapidly building two-sided density in a discovery infrastructure by using institutions that already hold trust relationships with the long tail — rather than mobilising participants one by one through field work.

An ecosystem aggregator is an existing institution (ITI, MSME association, NGO, skilling centre, cooperative) whose constituents are the exact population that national platforms cannot reach. Instead of each constituent joining individually, the aggregator onboards its entire constituency en masse — via bulk voice upload, QR code, or assisted voice — bringing hundreds of participants to the shared map in days rather than months.

The key mechanism: the aggregator does not need to explain the technology to each participant. It leverages the trust relationship that already exists between it and its constituents. The endorsement of a known institution carries weight that a government portal or direct outreach promotion does not.

Source: Tushar Bansal, EkStep Foundation, "Blue Dots AI — An AI Diffusion Pathway for Livelihoods," May 2026. 🟡

## Why it matters

A two-sided marketplace built person by person is expensive, slow, and never reaches density before early participants lose confidence. Without sufficient density on both sides, matches are poor, attrition is high, and the infrastructure fails before it can demonstrate value.

The aggregator model solves the cold start density problem. Instead of the facilitation team acquiring one seeker and one employer at a time, a single MSME association brings 300 employers in two weeks. A single ITI brings 500 job-seekers. The map reaches density before either side has reason to doubt it.

The model also determines who is included: aggregators serve the long tail by definition. An ITI's students are often first-generation graduates. An MSME association's members are the SMBs invisible on national platforms. The aggregator is the access point to exactly the population that discovery failure hits hardest.

## What the pathways show

🟡 **Dharwad (Karnataka, India):** One MSME association onboarded 300+ employers in 2 weeks. One ITI onboarded 500+ seekers. These two aggregators, on opposite sides of the livelihoods use case, were sufficient to establish density. The map then started generating matches on its own.

⬜ The aggregator model generalises across use cases: for livelihoods, the minimum viable pairing is one ITI or college (seeker side) and one MSME association (provider side). For other use cases — welfare, agriculture, tourism — the structure is one strong institution on the supply side and one on the demand side. These are institutions that already serve the long tail. The task is not to create them but to find them.

⬜ The Aggregator DPG gives each aggregator the tools to register, onboard participants at scale, and track each participant's status — active, at-risk, satisfied, stalled — so no participant goes cold without intervention. Aggregators also issue digitally verifiable credentials that enable a receiver to verify a participant in seconds, reducing transaction cost across the ecosystem.

## Key contrast with other aggregation models

⬜ The livelihoods aggregator model (two anchoring institutions, one on each side of a use case) is structurally different from the 54-enabler model documented in the agriculture ecosystem (see [enabler-ecosystem-assembly](../pathways/enabler-ecosystem-assembly.md)). In agriculture, the OAN assembles 54 enablers across four layers (institutional/governance, tech/AI, structured data, knowledge/docs). In the livelihoods discovery model, the minimum viable structure is two aggregating institutions — one per side — with additional aggregators layered in as density grows. The density logic differs: in agriculture, the knowledge network comes first; in livelihoods discovery, the two-sided density threshold comes first.

## Critical failure mode: the map goes stale

🟡 Aggregators alone do not sustain the ecosystem. Blue Dots go stale: participants move, vacancies fill, seekers find work or drop out. Without a District Facilitation Team (6–8 people) maintaining weekly discovery rhythm, both sides lose confidence and the map reverts to a dormant database. The aggregator model builds density rapidly; the facilitation team maintains it. Remove the facilitation function and the aggregator's initial onboarding becomes a one-time event rather than a living ecosystem.
