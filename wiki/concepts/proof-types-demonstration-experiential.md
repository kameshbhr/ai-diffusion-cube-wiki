# Proof Types: Demonstration and Experiential

**Dimension(s):** A: Problem orientation (A4 Proof)
**Type:** Framework / Decision

## What this is

There are two types of proof that matter in AI deployment, and both are necessary:

**Demonstration proof** — showing a sceptical leader that AI works on a real problem, in a real setting, with real users. Demonstration proof triggers the decision to start. It must be product-independent: the moment you tie it to one vendor's platform, you have proved that their product works, not that the approach works.

**Experiential proof** — the leader having deployed it themselves and emerged with a different understanding of their own organisation. Experiential proof creates the champion who can sell internally and to others. A slide deck cannot produce this conversion.

These are not stages of the same process. They answer different questions and produce different outcomes. Demonstration proof answers "does this work?" Experiential proof answers "do I now understand what my own organisation could do with this?" Both are required.

## Why it matters

Most AI deployments invest heavily in demonstration proof (conference demos, benchmarks, case studies) and neglect experiential proof. The result is sceptical leaders who have seen something work for someone else but have no internal champion who has the personal conviction that comes from having deployed it themselves.

Conversely, an institution that tries to generate experiential proof without adequate demonstration proof risks investing significant institutional capital in a deployment that fails — undermining trust in the approach for a generation.

The sequence matters: demonstration proof → decision to start → experiential proof → internal champion.

## What the pathways show

🟡 The Amul CEO moved from "data is the new oil, I don't understand what that means" to "after I saw data coming together, I started seeing the magic" — not through a presentation but through seeing AI work on his own data, with his own operations. A slide deck could not have produced that conversion.

🟡 Proof from five different institutional types (a state government department, a national ministry, a cooperative, a state government with an independent build, and a national transformation institute in a different country) demonstrates that the pathway is not context-specific. Variety of proof contexts is itself a form of proof. 

🟡 In curating 67 AI impact stories for the AI Commons, the team found that more than half the builders could not articulate their own problem statement — it had to be written for them after interviews. A pathway's first function must be helping the adopter formulate their problem. A problem well-stated is a pathway half-found.

⬜ The most powerful demonstration proof is the compression sequence itself — 9 months (MahaVISTAAR) → 3 months (Ethiopia) → 3 weeks (Amul). The claim is not "the system works." The claim is "the pathway makes each next adoption faster." See [[concepts/compression-sequence]].

## Key decisions for adopters

| Decision | Options | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|---|
| What counts as adequate demonstration proof? | Benchmarks, conference demos, vendor results; OR working in real setting with real users, product-independent | Only the latter convinces sceptical leaders and enables diffusion. Benchmark-only proof ties adoption to one vendor's platform |
| In what sequence to generate the two proof types? | Demonstration first, experiential second | Demonstrated across all documented deployments in this source |
| What is the minimum viable demonstration? | Large-scale vs bounded pilot | Bounded ambition (one or two crops, a few districts) produces the early proof that enables scaling without requiring full commitment upfront |
