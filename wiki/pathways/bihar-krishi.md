# Bihar Krishi — Pathway

**Deployment:** Bihar Krishi — Bihar State Agriculture Platform
**Contributor:** Government of Bihar; MicroSave Consulting; Gates Foundation; EkStep Foundation (via Bharat-VISTAAR connection)
**Sector:** Agriculture
**Geography:** India — Bihar (all 38 districts)
**Actor type:** Government
**Journey stage:** Scaling
**Dimensions covered:** A, C, D, F
**Horizontal or vertical:** Vertical (sector-specific — agriculture)
**Deployment status:** Active
**Last updated:** 2026-06-02
**Contact for peer connection:** Government of Bihar Agriculture Department

## Summary

Bihar Krishi is Bihar's state agriculture platform, built independently under Bihar's 4th Agriculture Roadmap with support from MicroSave Consulting and the Gates Foundation, then connected to the national Bharat-VISTAAR layer when it launched. Bihar deployed against severe baseline constraints — historically one agriculture field officer for every 2,000 farmers — and has reached 850,000+ farmers across all 38 districts, trained 15,000+ extension workers, and onboarded 50+ government schemes, earning two national awards (ET DigiTech Gold, SKOCH Gold 2025). It demonstrates that the OAN pathway works when built independently and connected retroactively, not only when built on OAN DPGs from the start.

## A — Problem Orientation

*What you build on.*

**Who were you trying to serve, and what specific problem were you solving for them?**
Bihar's smallholder farmers — historically underserved, with roughly one agriculture field officer for every 2,000 farmers. A predominantly rural economy with deep exclusion from timely advisory, scheme access, and market information. 25% of the farmer population are women. Bihar Krishi was designed to address the structural gap in extension capacity and scheme accessibility.

**Who defined the problem — the deployer, the institution, or the user — and how do you know the user agrees?**
The Government of Bihar defined the problem through the 4th Agriculture Roadmap process. Evidence of user agreement comes from outcomes: 850,000+ farmers registered, 38,000+ scheme applications submitted, 20–25% monthly engagement rate.

**How did you define your success metrics — are they usage based or outcome based?**
Farmer registration (850,000+), scheme application submission (38,000+), monthly engagement rate (20–25%), district coverage (all 38 districts), extension worker training (15,000+). Two national awards (ET DigiTech Gold, SKOCH Gold 2025) indicate quality recognition beyond usage metrics. Full agronomic outcome metrics are not documented in available sources.

**Did you discover something in the field that you hadn't anticipated?**
Not documented in available sources.

**Was there data already available to start with, or did you have to build or collect it first?**
Bihar had limited digital advisory infrastructure compared to Maharashtra. The 4th Agriculture Roadmap process involved significant data assembly — 50+ government schemes unified on a single platform, 15,000+ extension workers trained and connected. The data foundation was built as part of the deployment rather than inherited.

**Why did this problem need AI — what would a non-AI solution have missed?**
At one field officer per 2,000 farmers, the extension system cannot reach most farmers with timely advisory. AI enables advisory at scale without proportional staff expansion. The scheme access problem (38,000+ applications submitted through the platform) also required a system that could navigate 50+ schemes on behalf of farmers who previously had no accessible guide.

**What were the access constraints of your users — language, literacy, connectivity — and how did that shape what you built?**
Bihar's farmers face language barriers (Hindi is primary, with regional variation), literacy constraints, and limited connectivity. The specific access design of Bihar Krishi — which channels it uses, whether voice-first or app-first — is not documented in detail in available sources. The 20 million farmers reached through digital outreach (versus 850,000 registered users) suggests multiple channels including broadcast mechanisms.

**Did you design the system to wait for users to come to it, or did it reach out to them too?**
Not documented in detail. The 20 million farmers reached through digital outreach suggests proactive outbound was used for awareness and mobilisation even if not for personalised advisory.

## E — Workforce

*Who absorbs AI.*

**Were there people — field workers, extension officers, call centre staff — whose job changed?**
Yes, significantly. 15,000+ extension workers across 38 districts were trained. Given Bihar's historical baseline of one officer per 2,000 farmers, AI changes the extension officer's role from the primary (and scarce) source of advice to a facilitator who handles cases the AI cannot resolve.

**When the AI gave an answer or recommendation to a user, what was the last-mile human expected to do with it?**
Not documented in detail. The 15,000+ trained extension workers suggest a human-in-the-loop model where AI provides advisory and extension workers handle escalation and scheme facilitation.

**How and when were they brought in, and what did they need to learn?**
15,000+ extension workers trained across 38 districts — the Six Shifts framework (E1, E2) cites Bihar Krishi specifically as the example of structured, deliberate, scaled workforce training for an institutional deployment. The training was not bolted on at the end but was integral to the rollout. Specific training content and timing are not documented.
